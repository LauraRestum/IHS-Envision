AbilityOne is not a charity program; it is a procurement program with the force of federal law behind it. Congress created it through the Javits-Wagner-O'Day Act, and an independent federal agency, the U.S. AbilityOne Commission, runs it. When an item is placed on the AbilityOne Procurement List, it becomes a required source for federal purchases. Roughly 41,000 people work through the program, and it delivered more than 4.7 billion dollars in products and services to the government in fiscal 2025. Source: U.S. AbilityOne Commission FY2025 Performance and Accountability Report.

Authority, from the top down

How AbilityOne works

Chart takeaway: The President appoints the 15-member U.S. AbilityOne Commission, 11 federal agency representatives and 4 private citizens, operating under authority Congress granted in the Javits-Wagner-O'Day Act. The Commission designates two central nonprofit agencies, National Industries for the Blind and SourceAmerica, which is the former NISH, renamed in 2013. Those two organizations coordinate a national network of nonprofit agencies. Envision is one of the NIB-affiliated agencies. The output of the whole structure flows to federal customers, including VA and IHS. The Commission also approves each item added to the Procurement List and sets its fair market price.

A 60-second materials lesson

Know your liners

Pick a scenario

, high density

  • Thin gauge with high strength for its weight
  • Best for routine dry waste
  • Economical case yields for high-volume areas

, linear low density

  • Stretch, puncture and tear resistance
  • Best for heavy, wet or sharp-edged waste
  • The workhorse for clinical and food service areas

Both families are on contract, and the full Envision line runs wider: color-coded medical isolation liners, compostable options, and custom sizes, colors and in-line imprinting, manufactured end to end at our Wichita campus. We help your teams match the liner to the application and right-size to cut cost and waste.

A 60-second materials lesson, because it saves facilities real money. High-density polyethylene is thin and strong for its weight; it excels at routine dry waste and gives you more liners per case. Linear low-density stretches and resists punctures and tears; it is what you want where waste is heavy, wet, or has sharp edges. Most facilities over-spec one and under-spec the other. Part of what we offer is walking your environmental services teams through exactly this, so you buy once, correctly. If someone asks deeper technical questions, offer the on-site demonstration and samples rather than improvising specs live.

Two authorities, one rulebook

AbilityOne and the Buy Indian Act work together

  • Your Buy Indian Act priority stays fully intact; it governs the wide universe of goods and services not on the AbilityOne Procurement List
  • For the narrow set of items that are on the Procurement List, federal rules already made the sourcing decision
  • Ordering those items through MSPV is compliant by default, and keeps Buy Indian authority focused where it reaches furthest
  • Our own supply chain includes Dakota Western, which supplies film for our can liner production

From the IHS acquisition regulation

“The Buy Indian Act does not apply when mandatory government sources are available, as required by FAR 8.002.”
Subpart 326.6

TALK TRACK (use this framing, it is accurate and it respects the room): We know how much Buy Indian Act priority matters here, and nothing about this program touches it. The Buy Indian Act governs the vast majority of what IHS buys, everything not on the AbilityOne Procurement List, and that stays exactly as it is. For the narrow set of items that are on the Procurement List, IHS's own regulation, HHSAR 326.6, says the Act does not apply where mandatory government sources exist, per FAR 8.002. So choosing AbilityOne items through MSPV is not choosing against Native-owned business. It is using the already-decided, lower-risk pathway for those specific commodities, which frees your Buy Indian authority for the awards where it has the greatest reach. If pressed on precedence: the correct, precise claim is item-specific, "for items on the Procurement List," never a blanket "AbilityOne outranks Buy Indian."
